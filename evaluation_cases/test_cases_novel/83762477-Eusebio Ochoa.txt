Eusebio Ochoa Isaza (2 October 1880 – 24 September 1955) was a Colombian musician and composer of pasillos and bambucos. In July 1910 he recorded in New York with the ensemble La Lira Antioqueña for the Columbia Phonograph Company; the sessions included a version of the Colombian national anthem often described in Colombia as the anthem's first phonographic recording. His best-known composition is the bambuco "El profesor de canto". He later taught at the Institute of Fine Arts of Medellín, where he became dean of the teaching staff.


== Early life ==
Eusebio Ochoa Isaza was born in Concepción, Antioquia, on 2 October 1880. He came from a family of musicians: his father sang in a church choir in Envigado and his mother sang and played the guitar. The family settled in Medellín in 1886, where he completed primary school and part of his secondary schooling, and he also spent time in Manizales, Cali and Barranquilla. After returning to Medellín he served in the government forces during the Thousand Days' War, until 1901; his first known song, "Virgencita, me voy a la guerra" ("Little Virgin, I am leaving for the war"), dates from this period.


== La Lira Antioqueña and the New York recordings ==

In 1903 Ochoa joined La Lira Antioqueña, a string ensemble founded that year in Medellín. In July 1910 the group travelled to New York City after signing a contract with the Columbia Phonograph Company (later Columbia Records) to record Colombian music. The Medellín newspaper El Mundo describes these as the first recordings of Colombian music made in New York.
The Discography of American Historical Recordings documents 32 Columbia masters listed under the group's name, all made in 1910 and mostly ten-inch, including "Himno nacional de Colombia" (matrix 4720), "Amores" (matrix 4701, recorded in June or July 1910), and the waltz "Flor de lis" (matrix 4653). The ensemble's singers on the New York sessions were Ochoa himself, Leonel Calle and Enrique Gutiérrez, the latter known as "Cabecitas"; releases of their duets were credited to "Calle y Ochoa" and "Cabecitas y Ochoa". Ten-inch, 78 rpm Columbia discs of the ensemble from this period are held by the Fonoteca Departamental Hernán Restrepo Duque in Medellín, among them the issues C-868 and C-919.
One of the discs issued from the sessions, Columbia C875, contained an instrumental version of the Colombian national anthem, released for the centenary of Colombian independence; El Colombiano calls it the anthem's first phonographic recording. Colombian music historian Jaime Rico Salazar notes, however, that an earlier instrumental version had been recorded by Arthur Pryor's band for Victor (catalogue 62884) on 9 June 1910; he dates the Lira Antioqueña version, directed by composer Emilio Murillo and backed with the waltz "Flor de lys", to 20 July 1910.


== Later career ==
On his return to Medellín, Ochoa played the guitar with the ensemble Lira Unión, and in 1911 he entered the city's Institute of Fine Arts (Instituto de Bellas Artes), which had begun operating that year and in 1926 became the Palacio de Bellas Artes. There he studied music theory and solfège with the Spanish musician Jesús Arriola and the double bass with Luis Mondragón, and he later taught at the institute, becoming dean of the teaching staff. He played the double bass in the Banda Departamental and in several of the city's orchestras, and was choir master of the Metropolitan Cathedral of Medellín.
Ochoa composed pasillos and bambucos, but he signed his works with the pseudonyms "Pepe" and "Alejandro Leprevost", or left them unsigned, and little of his work as a composer is known today. His best-known work is the bambuco "El profesor de canto" ("The singing teacher").


== Death and family ==
Ochoa died in Medellín on 24 September 1955. Music historian Jaime Rico Salazar writes that Ochoa married twice and had 18 children; his second wife was Flor María Cárdenas, mother of the composer Héctor Ochoa Cárdenas, known for the song "El camino de la vida".
