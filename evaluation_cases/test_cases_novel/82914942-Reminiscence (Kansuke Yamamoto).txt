Reminiscence (追憶, Tsuioku) is a 1953 photograph by the Japanese photographer and poet Kansuke Yamamoto. It has been discussed as part of Yamamoto's recurring birdcage imagery, a motif that connects the work to both his prewar and postwar Surrealist practice. The photograph was included in the Getty Museum's 2013 exhibition Japan's Modern Divide: The Photographs of Hiroshi Hamaya and Kansuke Yamamoto, shown again in a 2016 Yamamoto exhibition at Taka Ishii Gallery New York, and featured in Sotheby's 2019 Classic Photographs sale.


== Description ==
The work has been described variously as a gelatin silver print, a photomontage, and a multiple-exposure work. It was also included by amanasalto among fourteen early Yamamoto masterpieces selected from works made between 1933 and 1953. Sotheby's described the auctioned print as a 1953 photograph, possibly ferrotyped, measuring 12 1/4 by 10 inches (31.1 by 25.4 cm), with annotations by Toshio Yamamoto on the reverse and mount.


== Interpretation ==
According to Taka Ishii Gallery, the image superimposes the twisted bars of a birdcage over a Japanese city, and the result "unmistakably" recalls the memory of the atomic bomb. Sotheby's similarly described the work as overlaying a mangled, burned cage on a Japanese cityscape, calling it evocative of the devastating aftermath of the atomic bomb. In Sotheby's reading, the absent bird also introduces a note of hope, since no creature is visibly trapped inside the cage.
A more cautious reading appears in Eiko Aoki's interview with the Getty curators in Kyoto Journal. There, the work is discussed as one that may evoke the aftermath of the atomic bomb, but the curators stop short of saying that the birdcage motif itself directly signified the bomb for Yamamoto. Instead, the interview stresses the broader importance of the birdcage as a recurring image in his work.


== Place in Yamamoto's work ==
The birdcage is one of the most persistent motifs in Yamamoto's photography and poetry. In Amanda Maddox's essay for the Getty catalogue, the 1940 work Buddhist Temple's Birdcage is treated as a key example of Yamamoto's use of the cage in connection with silence, regulation, and free expression under wartime conditions. Maddox argues that Yamamoto repeatedly used the motif to register oppression while preserving the possibility of release or escape.
The same catalogue places Yamamoto's broader practice beyond simple imitation of European Surrealism. Maddox writes that he should be understood not as a derivative follower but as an interpreter who expanded Surrealist forms and themes within the context of modern Japan. In that context, Reminiscence has been treated as a postwar extension of Yamamoto's birdcage imagery rather than an isolated late work.
Ryūichi Kaneko, in the same volume, argues that Yamamoto's postwar work has often been marginalized in standard histories of Japanese photography, even though those later works show "increased complexity and strength". Reminiscence belongs to that postwar body of work.


== Exhibition history ==
Reminiscence was shown in 2013 in Japan's Modern Divide: The Photographs of Hiroshi Hamaya and Kansuke Yamamoto at the J. Paul Getty Museum. The Getty's exhibition page presents it as part of Yamamoto's experimental work from the late 1940s through the early 1960s. In 2016 the work was included in Taka Ishii Gallery New York's Yamamoto exhibition, where it was singled out as one of fourteen iconic images from 1933 to 1953. amanasalto later included it in its "Selection from Early Works (1933-1953)" reproduction project supervised by Toshio Yamamoto.


== Auction history ==
On 3 October 2019, Reminiscence was offered as lot 198 in Sotheby's Classic Photographs auction in New York. Sotheby's later selected the work for its year-end feature "Sotheby's Most Memorable Photographs Sold in 2019". ArtDaily reported that the sale set a new auction record for Yamamoto.
